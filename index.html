How to Clip Your Nails

A quick, no-fuss guide to trimming fingernails and toenails cleanly and safely.

Supplies Fingernails Toenails Tips Avoid

🧰 What you'll need

  • Nail clippers: a smaller curved pair for fingers, a larger straight pair for toes
  • Nail file or emery board to smooth rough edges
  • A towel or trash can to catch clippings
  • Good lighting
  • Optional: a bowl of warm water, cuticle oil, or hand lotion

✋ Clipping fingernails

  1. Soften the nails. Clip after a shower, or soak your hands in warm water for a few minutes. Soft nails cut cleaner and are less likely to split.
  2. Pick your length. Leave about 1–2 mm of white at the tip. Cutting down to the skin can hurt and invite infection.
  3. Clip in small bites. Start at one side and make several small cuts across the nail rather than one big snip. This keeps the nail from cracking.
  4. Follow a gentle curve. Fingernails look and feel best following the natural rounded shape of your fingertip.
  5. File the edges. File in one direction (not back and forth) to smooth any sharp corners or snags.
  6. Moisturize. Rub in a bit of lotion or cuticle oil to keep nails and skin healthy.

🦶 Clipping toenails

  1. Soak your feet. Toenails are thicker, so 5–10 minutes in warm water makes a big difference.
  2. Dry thoroughly. Wet nails can bend instead of cutting cleanly.
  3. Cut straight across. This is the most important rule for toes. Don't round the corners. Straight edges help prevent ingrown toenails.
  4. Don't go too short. Keep the nail level with the tip of your toe.
  5. Lightly file the corners. Just enough to remove sharpness, while keeping the straight shape.

✨ Helpful tips

  • How often? Fingernails roughly every 1–2 weeks; toenails every 3–6 weeks.
  • Keep tools clean. Wipe clippers with rubbing alcohol before and after use, and don't share them.
  • Use your dominant hand last. Clipping with your non-dominant hand is awkward; take it slow.
  • Sharp clippers matter. Dull clippers crush and tear nails. Replace them when they stop cutting cleanly.
  • Kids and pets are best done when they're calm or sleepy, with smaller, rounded-tip clippers.

⚠️ What to avoid

  • Cutting too short or into the skin
  • Ripping or biting off hangnails; clip them neatly instead
  • Digging into the corners of toenails
  • Cutting cuticles; gently push them back instead

If you have diabetes, poor circulation, very thick nails, or signs of infection (redness, swelling, pus), see a doctor or podiatrist for nail care.

Happy trimming! ✂️
